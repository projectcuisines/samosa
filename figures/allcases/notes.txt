Energy Balance Figure (fig_energy_balance.py, added 2026-08-13):

  Plots (OLR - ASR)/(S/4) x 100 %, i.e. the residual TOA imbalance as a percentage of the incident stellar flux. S/4 is fixed by the protocol, so the
  denominator is identical across models and the comparison is exact. Positive = still cooling. Symlog y-axis, linear inside +/-1%.

  Sources: ExoCAM output.txt (OLR, toaEBAL; ASR = OLR + toaEBAL); ExoPlaSim samosaNN.nc area-weighted rst/rlut (note rsut and rlut are stored negative);
  ROCKE-3D rocke_NNq.nc -trnf_toa_hemis[2]/srnf_toa_hemis[2]; Generic PCM and LFRic from their .dat/.txt global output; PlaHab from
  simulations/sampleN/ (NOT the top-level global_samosa_plahab_seq1sam4.dat, which is a stale duplicate disagreeing by ~3 K in Tglob).

  Result: only the Generic PCM has no accepted case within 1% (median 3.3%, max 19.7% at Case 10). Everything else is mostly inside 1%. The four
  ROCKE-3D cases above 3% (2, 6, 10, 12) are exactly the four flagged in the ROCKE-3D README as unstable or as retaining an imbalance.

  Caveat: TOA balance is necessary but not sufficient for equilibrium -- a run can be balanced at TOA while the slab ocean still drifts.

  ExoCAM Case 7: exocam/samosa7.cam.h0.avg.nc exists with real data (TS=345.1 K, ICEFRAC=0, ASR=309.5, OLR=288.4 -> -5.28%) but has no row in
  exocam/output.txt. It is a runaway in progress, so excluding it from the analysis is right, but it belongs on this figure as an open marker and was
  missing from the first version. Its value comes from FSNT/FLUT rather than toaEBAL.

  ExoCAM archive gap: in the submitted samosaN.cam.h0.avg.nc files, FSNTOA, FSNTOAC, FSNTC, FSDSC, FLUTC, SWCF and LWCF are ALL identically zero.
  Only FSNT, FSDS, FLUT and FLNT carry data. So ExoCAM's TOA shortwave cannot be checked independently; FSNT is top-of-model and sits ~1 W/m^2 from
  the reported toaEBAL (cases 11 and 12 differ most: 1.31% vs 0.70% and 0.91% vs 0.20%). Worth requesting a resubmission with the TOA and clear-sky
  fields populated -- it would also make cloud radiative forcing available to the intercomparison.

  LFRic verification: lfric_samosa_caseNN.nc sw_net_toa and lw_up_toa reproduce the .txt global diagnostics exactly (ASR, OLR, albedo, imbalance all

Albedo Figure (fig_interpolation_albedo.py, added 2026-08-13):

  Same 6-panel kriging layout as the other interpolation figures, logit-space like cloud fraction, runaways excluded. Albedo taken as reported where
  available (ExoCAM TOAALB, ROCKE-3D plan_alb) and otherwise 1 - ASR/(S/4); the two agree to <0.01 pp for ROCKE-3D Case 1.

  Color scale truncated to 10-45% (extend='both') because the full 0-70% range washes out the contrast. sigma_threshold=1.0 logit units only trips for
  PlaHab, whose variogram is inflated by two extreme points (0.9% at Case 10, 63.7% at Case 11); the Case 10 value looks wrong for a 190 K surface and
  that case also carries a -9.7% energy imbalance.

  Result: albedo spread (median 19.9 pp) is about half the cloud fraction spread (median 41.9 pp), but the model ranking is still unstable --
  ExoPlaSim is brightest at 6 sample points and darkest at 4.

  DEGENERATE PANELS: the linear variogram fits slope=0 exactly for ExoCAM and LFRic, i.e. a pure nugget. Ordinary kriging then weights all points
  equally regardless of distance and returns the sample mean everywhere, so those panels are flat and carry no spatial information; only the
  exact_values=True pinning at the sample points survives. They are now stippled and labelled in the figure. This was NOT caught by reusing the
  linear variogram from the other interpolation figures -- crossval_variogram_albedo.py was written afterwards to check it.

  crossval_variogram_albedo.py: linear still wins on mean LOO RMSE (0.461 vs 0.468 exponential, 0.479 spherical, 0.482 power, 0.484 gaussian), so it
  remains the right global choice. Per model: for ExoCAM every non-degenerate alternative is worse (0.374-0.401 vs 0.360), so a constant really is the
  best estimate there. For LFRic (only 6 points) exponential does better (0.297 vs 0.334), so its flatness is partly a fitting artifact.

  Related pre-existing issue: in fig_interpolation_clouds.py the Generic PCM fit has slope=0.0079, i.e. very nearly degenerate too (field ptp 0.89
  logit vs 2.3-4.8 for the other models). That panel of the cloud fraction figure is close to flat for the same reason and is worth revisiting.


Standard Deviation Figure:

  Spread metric                                                                                                                                                                                                     
  Each panel shows the variance-weighted inter-model standard deviation across the full (instellation, pN₂) parameter space. For surface temperature the std is in K; for water vapor it is in dex (σ of log₁₀ of   
  the column in kg m⁻²); for cloud fraction it is in %.                                                                                                                                                             
                                                            
  Kriging                                                                                                                                                                                                           
  Each model's data were independently interpolated onto the common grid using Ordinary Kriging with a linear variogram, with axes normalized to [0,1] before fitting. Water vapor was kriged in natural-log space;
  cloud fraction in logit space (then back-transformed via sigmoid to % before computing the spread).                                                                                                               
                                                            
  Runaway exclusion                                                                                                                                                                                                 
  Cases flagged as runaway greenhouse (sentinel values: 600 K for temperature, 10⁴ kg m⁻² for water vapor, 200% for cloud fraction) were excluded from kriging for all variables. PlaHab provides no water vapor
  data and is excluded from that panel only.                                                                                                                                                                        
  
  Variance weighting                                                                                                                                                                                                
  Rather than a simple standard deviation, each model's contribution is weighted by the inverse of its kriging variance (w_i = 1/σ²_i), so models that are interpolating near actual sample points carry more weight
   than those extrapolating into unsampled regions. To prevent sample points from receiving arbitrarily large weight (kriging variance is exactly zero at exact data locations with exact_values=True), the variance
   floor is capped at 10% of the median kriging variance across all models and grid points, bounding the maximum weight ratio at approximately 10:1.
                                                                                                                                                                                                                    
  Single-model masking                                      
  Hatched regions indicate areas where only one model (ExoPlaSim) has valid sample data — specifically the three QMC sample points at (1900 W m⁻², 7.85 bar), (2400 W m⁻², 0.21 bar), and (2100 W m⁻², 1.83 bar),
  which are runaway cases for ExoCAM, ROCKE-3D, and PlaHab and outside the sampling subsets of Generic PCM and LFRic. The boundary was determined by kriging the per-point model count and masking where the        
  interpolated count falls below 1.5. Sample points are shown as open circles (multiple models) or × marks (single model only).
